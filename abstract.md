We state the multicommodity flow–cut theorems of Leighton and Rao for
uniform and product demands in undirected and directed networks. Maximum
concurrent fractional flow is within a logarithmic factor of the sparsest
cut, and a logarithmic loss is necessary for some uniform instances. The
product bound depends on the number of vertices with positive demand weight,
including when other vertices serve only as transit vertices.

The uniform short-path theorem gives a flow of value
$\Omega(\mathcal S/\log n)$ whose paths have length
$O(C_{\max}\log n/(n\mathcal S))$. Constructive guarantees for sparse
cuts and uniform short-path flows are stated for rational inputs using the
existing polynomial-time word-RAM model, with exact cut and fractional-flow
outputs and input size measured in bits.

The elementary cut bound, the logarithmic converses, the matching lower
bound, and the short-path guarantee are separate statements. The two uniform
converses are derived from their product counterparts; the substantive
flow–cut and algorithmic proof obligations remain open.
